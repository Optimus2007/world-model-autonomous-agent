# Related work and novelty assessment

Compiled on 2026-10-28. Bibliographic details were checked against Crossref, the arXiv API, PMLR, and the
ICLR 2019 virtual programme. The access column records what was actually read:

- **Full text:** the paper itself (or its arXiv author version) was read in the sections relevant here.
- **Thesis:** the method was read in the first author's open PhD thesis, not in the journal version.
- **Abstract:** only the abstract was read.
- **Metadata + secondary:** bibliographic metadata was verified, but the paper's content is known only
  from another verified paper's description of it.

Paywalled copies were not accessed by any other route.

## 2. Does prior work already handle interrupted adaptation or recurring dynamics?

**Recurring dynamics: yes, by a different design.** MBCD (row 5), MOLe (row 7), the Gaussian-process
mixture of Xu et al. (row 9) and RL-CD (row 6) all keep one model per context and reuse it when the context
recurs. Our agent keeps a single model and relies on replay to slow forgetting. It has no notion of a
context identity. Any claim that our agent "handles recurrence" must therefore be read as "retains enough of
the old dynamics in one model", which is weaker than these methods. A library of per-context models is the
natural stronger baseline. It was not implemented here; Section 8 of the report (Limitations) discusses it.

**A change during an ongoing adaptation: yes, implicitly, in the same works.** A per-step context assignment
(MOLe, Xu et al.) has no adaptation window to interrupt. MBCD keeps detecting after a warm-up period and
starts or selects another model, so post-change data never contaminate the previous context's dataset.
Stream-learning methods with adaptive windows (ADWIN) or warning and drift levels (DDM) make the same two
moves: discard stale data on detected change, and do not trust detection until enough recent samples exist.

**What the literature does not report, as far as these sources show:** a controlled measurement of what goes
wrong when a common simple pipeline (detector-triggered, windowed fine-tuning of a single ensemble world
model, with alarms suppressed while the window is open) meets a second change inside the window. In
particular, none of the sources reports how much of the damage comes from suppressing alarms rather than
from the window's length, or how the answer interacts with replay. This is a narrow gap. It is an
evaluation question about a specific, widely used engineering pattern, not an open algorithmic problem.

## 3. Consequences for the claimed contribution

1. The restart policy implemented in Step 7 is **not novel as a mechanism**. It applies known ideas
   (discard stale data on detected change; a warm-up guard before detection) to the single-model, windowed
   setting. The report presents it as such.
2. The defensible contribution is **empirical and methodological**: a controlled, paired comparison of
   interruption policies (ignore, restart, and a short-window ignore baseline) and of replay. It uses one
   fixed system with separate tuning and evaluation seeds, reports false restarts, cost and forgetting, and
   includes scenarios not used for tuning.
3. Per-context model libraries (MBCD, MOLe) are the strongest competing approach to recurrence. Not
   comparing against one is a limitation, and it is stated as such.
